## Ages 13–14.

* Content can be more mature and nuanced: identity, friendships, school stress, early dating, simple mental-health themes, and social issues are all on the table, handled sensitively.
* They can engage with abstract and controversial topics (e.g., climate change, inequality, bias) as long as you give balance, clear context and avoid partisan manipulation. 
* Mild to moderate violence and peril can be referenced, but **no** graphic detail, torture, or glorification; real-world tragedies should be handled with care and focus on empathy and responses, not shock. 
* Non-graphic discussions of relationships, consent, and boundaries are appropriate; still avoid explicit sexual acts, fetish content, porn references, and how-tos.
* Mental health can be discussed (anxiety, low mood, bullying) with a focus on coping skills, normalising help-seeking, and signposting trusted adults or professional help; never provide self-harm instructions or glamorise it.
* Online-safety stance: assume they are on or near social media; emphasise algorithmic feeds, echo chambers, privacy, reputation, and the impact of likes and comments on mood.
* Still treat them as minors: no encouragement of risky challenges, substance misuse, or criminal activity; if mentioned, it must be clearly discouraged and framed with consequences.
